There should be more laws and rules to regulate LLMs because these systems are now powerful enough to cause real-world harm at scale, yet they are still operating with too little accountability.

First, LLMs can spread misinformation, scams, and harmful advice instantly to millions of people. Unlike a human speaker, an LLM can generate convincing falsehoods at massive speed, making damage broader and faster. Laws are needed to require basic safeguards, transparency, and liability when companies deploy systems that mislead or endanger users.

Second, LLMs can reinforce bias, privacy violations, and discrimination. They are trained on huge datasets that may contain personal data, copyrighted material, and social biases. Without regulation, companies have weak incentives to clean data, protect privacy, or test for unfair outcomes. Rules can force responsible data use, independent audits, and clear standards for fairness.

Third, regulation is necessary because the market alone does not protect the public. AI companies are rewarded for speed, scale, and profit, not necessarily safety. Voluntary guidelines are not enough when the downside includes fraud, manipulation, job displacement, and security risks. Just as we regulate medicine, food, and finance when public harm is possible, LLMs should face proportionate legal oversight.

In short, more laws and rules do not mean stopping innovation. They mean making innovation trustworthy. Regulation will help ensure LLMs are safe, fair, transparent, and accountable before their harms become impossible to control.
